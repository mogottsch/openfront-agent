Land policy — v4.4 focus gate, v4.5 size guidance under evaluation

P1 · Preserve growth in the opening

Wait when reserves are depleted, but do not treat an active wilderness push as an automatic reason to wait. Compare its remaining size with available reserves; a small tail plus healthy home army can justify a modest new send. Available wilderness is not an instruction to attack blindly. Compare each option's post-send reserve.

Evidence and caveat

The fixed-capacity 30% guideline was removed after v4 confused it with a 30% attack fraction and depleted reserves. v4.1 avoided that collapse in a 30-call browser smoke, but in a paired Impossible-nation 30-second engine run it selected five wilderness attacks and reached only 2,370 tiles. v4.2 selected 17 small wilderness sends and reached 4,828 tiles on the same seed, with 10,568 display troops retained. The aggressive fixed-20% baseline reached 5,237 tiles but retained only 1,088 troops. All runs were censored before an engine win event, so these are opening tradeoffs, not win-rate evidence.

P2 · Finish the tribe; collect its gold

Moritz's latest rule: once we attack a tribe, finish that tribe before opening another attack front. Partial territory yields no conquest gold; eliminating the tribe does. Clear bordering tribes before attacking any nation. When no tribe is underway, wilderness remains an option but weak/contested tribes can be worth prioritizing for gold. A tribe already attacked by another human/nation is a gold-steal opportunity, never a reason to abandon our own unfinished target.

Source and measured failure

Source-grounded Bot conquest transfers the full remaining Bot wallet to the killer once owned tiles fall below 100; a partial attack pays zero conquest gold (mechanics and active-TribeExecution sizing). In a real bounded Easy match, Jev locked to tribe50 for six 10% sends and never opened a nation or another tribe front, but the tribe remained alive and grew from 1,925 to 3,145 tiles while Jev waited between attacks (exact model/intent/game evidence). The next iteration supplies factual progress history; it does not force 20%.

P3 · Tribe commitment ceiling: 20%

Moritz's rule: never commit more than 20% against a tribe during this stage. Population growth is more important than forcing a one-send finish. Prefer weak tribes, and wait to rebuild before sending again when needed. The proposed large-send tribe rule is dropped.

Implementation boundary

Use wait/10%/20% for tribes. The broader 30/40/50% menu remains useful for other target types. The one-send benchmark below is mechanical evidence, not a recommendation to exceed this ceiling. The ≤20% tribe cap is enforced in offered choices and rechecked against the live target type at execution. An active-TribeExecution controlled comparison found 20% follow-ups sometimes finished when repeated 10% did not, but no universal send or guaranteed capture ratio was established.

P4 · Preserve reserves under pressure

Moritz's rule: do not full-send under attack. Usually absorb pressure until stronger than the attacker; attackers often draw attacks from other players themselves. No borrowed 75%/90% neighbor-reserve formula is being adopted.

Moritz resolved the definition

Compare our available reserve against the attacker's remaining reserve plus all active troops already attacking us; count that reserve only once across their attacks. At 20k versus 12k home plus 15k incoming, we are weaker than their combined 27k. City-defense emergencies remain outside the current land prompt.

P5 · Counterattack is not the default response

The intended defensive posture is patient absorption, not automatic troop-for-troop cancellation. A later counterattack still needs to preserve a usable reserve. Repeated large sends must not become an effective full-send simply because each individual action is below 100%.

Mechanics still apply

Reverse attacks first cancel opposing troops one-for-one. That fact alone does not tell us when counterattacking is strategically appropriate. v4.4 uses Moritz's combined-force comparison as a derived fact, not an automatic counterattack trigger.

P6 · Do not blindly top up an existing push

Before reinforcing, compare the target with the force we already have attacking it. Avoid repeatedly committing a new large fraction when the existing stack is sufficient. Outgoing land attack troops and retreat status are now observed; no terrain/post coverage is implied.

Deferred scope

Terrain and enemy Defense Post coverage are still not included. The normal land-attack intent still cannot select a particular border segment or route around a post. See hybrid spatial plan for separate future work.

P7 · Nations are a commitment, not a 10% probe

Moritz's rule: do not start a nation attack while any bordering tribe remains. Later, attack a nation only when a credible whole-conquest campaign is ready, ideally one decisive send; avoid merely damaging a stronger nation. This is a conservative strategic judgment, not a false universal ratio.

Source and boundary

In an actual-engine Impossible-nation pair, one small human attack immediately changed the defender from Neutral to Hostile; its first native counterattack was queued at tick 58 rather than 106 in the no-attack control. Relation slowly decays and is not literally permanent. Full nation conquest cannot be guaranteed from its current reserve/territory alone; terrain, posts, regeneration and retaliation remain uncertain. See primary-source test.

Bounded Easy live test: focus succeeds, conquest does not

One headed Europe Compact Solo Easy game, 400 tribes and five nations, 50 consecutive real Jev responses at one-second spacing, no manual attacks or Copilot. Outcomes: 19 10%-wilderness choices, 25 waits and six 10% land intents against one tribe50. No nation/new-tribe attack was selected after focus began, and a still-alive focus persisted after each attacking stack exhausted. Target tribe tiles rose 1,925→3,145 while our available reserve recovered 6,812→19,927 displayed troops; gold rose 26,200→47,300 but the target remained alive and no tribe50 conquest payout was observed. The last response gave 10% probability 0.47 versus 20% 0.43. This is a strategy-quality failure, not a code violation or completed match. Details and limits: bounded validation. A separate deterministic Europe Easy control collected 9,050 gold only upon one focused tribe kill but lost the five-minute timer (paired controls).

Reproduction and limits

npm run analyze:combat writes logs/combat-analysis.json. Different target geometry, active counters, terrain, posts and troop growth can change outcomes. These are ten deterministic cases, not general win probabilities. Do not compare remaining reserves across unequal elapsed times as if they were casualty counts.

What the current code establishes

  • Repeated attacks on the same target merge. Fresh sends are not independent parallel attacks.
  • Incoming reverse attacks cancel one-for-one before the remaining attack continues.
  • Human-versus-tribe combat applies a 0.7 multiplier to the terrain loss magnitude. Tribes are not equivalent to nations.
  • Defender casualties per conquered tile use current reserve / owned tiles; troop density and territorial scale matter.
  • Defense Post range is 30 tiles here; covered terrain loss magnitude is multiplied by 5 and its time cost by 3.
  • Normal land intents contain only a target player and troop amount. Directional routing is not an available action.

All claims above are from the checked-out source, not assumptions about every OpenFront release.

Confirmed decisions

  1. D1: Global land-action ceiling is 50%, in ten-point increments, plus wait; the tribe-specific D4 ceiling is enforced.
  2. D2: Land priorities have iterated from v4 to v4.4: the old fixed 30% phrase and v4.1 categorical active-push wait were removed after live failures. The model chooses each offered action; v4.4 withholds explicitly disallowed targets before inference and shows the reasons.
  3. D3: Local-solo scope, real-game legality and one-second single-flight remain. City/Defense Post/naval options are separately gated Hybrid features, not hidden land-policy actions.
  4. D4: Tribe commitments must stay at or below 20%; prefer waiting and population growth to a larger immediate send.
  5. D5: Wilderness is useful before a tribe fight, but early conquest gold matters: prefer a viable weak or human/nation-contested tribe when that payoff justifies fighting. Once we start one, finish that tribe before opening another attack front; other tribes' attacks do not create a gold-steal exception.
  6. D6: Usually absorb until our available reserve exceeds the attacker's reserve plus all its active incoming forces, preserving reserves and avoiding full-sends. A strength advantage does not force an attack.
  7. D7: Do not attack nations while bordering tribes remain. A nation attack later requires credible complete conquest, ideally one well-supported send; a token 10% raid on a stronger nation is not a harmless test.
  8. D8: Iterate on Easy first, then Medium/Hard/Impossible, evaluating observed conquest gold and whole-match wins—not merely mocked tests, Jev probabilities or sent intents.
