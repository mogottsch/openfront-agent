# Tribe conquest gold and native-nation hostility (OpenFront engine)

This note checks Moritz's live-play correction against the **local OpenFront engine**, not against a model judgment or a universal troop-size rule. Moritz wants to **finish a tribe and collect conquest gold before starting another attack**, clear bordering tribes before attacking nations, and attack a nation only with a credible whole-conquest plan because aggression provokes lasting hostility. Those are *user strategy goals*; the verified mechanics and uncertainties are distinguished below. Source checkout: `bb8af015b515b3b717bd4d901074c5f4c16641cb`. No browser game, Jev/Copilot request, sidecar call, upstream edit or controller action was used.

## Reproducible headless microbench

```sh
../OpenFrontIO/node_modules/.bin/tsx --tsconfig ../OpenFrontIO/tsconfig.json scripts/analyze-tribe-finish.mts
```

Two runs wrote byte-identical ignored `logs/tribe-gold-nation-hostility.json`. Both experiments use real `Config`/`GameRunner`/`Executor`/`AttackExecution` and normal stamped human attacks on separate fresh synthetic all-plains maps. The tribe is a **passive Bot with real `PlayerExecution` growth but no TribeExecution AI**; a controlled 100,000-gold stockpile is inserted before combat to make transfer visible. The nation has a **real Impossible `NationExecution`** after identical prepared ownership/starting armies, but no third-party player. This is a mechanical comparison, not a full match or win-rate test.

### Partial tribe attack: no conquest gold; elimination: full target wallet

A human with 2,500 tiles and 200,000 **internal** troops bordered a 1,000-tile Bot with 50,000 troops and the controlled 100,000 gold. The initial 8,000-troop land attack took tiles but did not eliminate the Bot. At game tick 17 the Bot had **939 tiles**, a still-growing wallet of **100,800 gold**, **no `ConquestEvent`**, and the human held **1,600 gold** solely from worker income (no loot). Later bounded scripted reinforcements brought the Bot below **100 tiles**. At the actual `conquerPlayer` call, Bot tiles were **99**, its wallet **102,100**, and the human wallet **4,200**; the single `ConquestEvent.gold` was **102,100**, the victim wallet dropped to **0**, and the human wallet rose to **106,300** in that same call. This measured full-wallet transfer is the **conquest bounty**, distinct from partial territorial gain or worker gold. The subsequent remnant redistribution left the Bot with zero tiles. Reinforcement sizes in this experiment are *not* an approved bot policy or a calculated finish threshold.

Source: `src/core/execution/AttackExecution.ts:435-460` calls `Game.conquerPlayer` when a target player's owned tiles fall **below 100**, not only at literal zero; `src/core/game/GameImpl.ts:1371-1449` credits the player whose attack crosses that threshold and emits one `ConquestEvent`. `src/core/configuration/Config.ts:724-733` gives **100% of a Bot/Nation's remaining gold** to the conqueror, **50% of a Human's**. `GameImpl.ts:1385-1399` has an exception: a never-attacking Human victim grants **zero** conquest gold (including when starting gold is enabled). `tests/ConquerGold.test.ts` covers those cases. Ordinary per-tile attrition does not call `conquerPlayer`; other game income sources can still change gold in a live game. In multi-attacker play, the attacker who triggers the elimination receives the transfer, so partial damage to a tribe is not a claim on its treasury. A later send cannot be justified from this one geometry: defender regeneration, terrain, connected borders, incoming third-party attacks, defense structures, target wallet and actual attack troops all matter.

### One nation attack: hostility before conquest, earlier native response

Two fresh 64×64 prepared FFA worlds had identical 2,048-tile human/nation halves, no wilderness contacts, and active PlayerExecution growth. At tick **56** the nation's relation to the human was **Neutral** in both. One branch made no human send; the other submitted a **single normal 5,000-internal-troop attack** on the nation at the next turn. At tick **57**, before a full conquest, the target nation had the incoming attack, its relation to the human was **Hostile**, and it had a temporary embargo. With only observation-only logging around `Game.addExecution`, the native nation first queued its own attack on the human at **tick 58** in the attacked case versus **tick 106** in the no-human-attack control. It queued subsequent attacks in both cases; merely recording a queued attack is **not** a separate proof that all its troops landed or that every attack did damage. In the attacked run the nation's relation was still **Hostile** at 10, 50 and 100 ticks after the initiating turn, even after that first incoming human attack had ended. The no-send control was Neutral at 50 ticks and Distrustful by 100 ticks after its *own* later combat. Both prepared scenarios eventually reached an engine Win event won by the nation (attacked at tick **241**, control at tick **311**), but this tiny deterministic example is **not** a general proof that one attack causes a loss.

The causal engine step is explicit: `src/core/execution/AttackExecution.ts:66-194` checks the target and, **at attack initialization**, applies a difficulty-specific relation change to the *defender*: **−100 on Impossible** (Easy −60, Medium −70, Hard −80) and adds a temporary embargo. It does not wait for territorial conquest. `src/core/game/PlayerImpl.ts:920-966` maps numeric relation below −50 to `Relation.Hostile`, clamps it to [−100,100], and decays it by only **0.05 per game tick** during `PlayerExecution.tick` (`PlayerExecution.ts:46-49`). From −100, crossing the Hostile threshold by decay alone would take around 1,000 ticks (~100 simulated seconds), **if no other relation/embargo interactions occur**. In actual native play, those interactions can change it: `NationExecution.ts:295-367` reacts to embargoes and hostility, and Impossible does not automatically stop its own embargo under the non-Impossible recovery branches. Do not label hatred literally permanent.

`src/core/execution/utils/AiAttackBehavior.ts:260-383,393-430` puts **retaliate first** in Impossible's strategy order when it finds a non-friendly *human/nation* incoming attack; bots' incoming attacks are ignored for a non-bot nation (`:420-449`). But `attackBestTarget` still checks the reserve/trigger ratios first (`:233-251,394-405`), `sendAttack` can fail legality and troop checks (`:778-797,904-989`), and native boats, relative army size, target eligibility, topology and timing can change the outcome. Thus a legal human nation attack reliably creates Hostile relation immediately at initialization, but a guaranteed retaliatory **send size, time, or win/loss threshold** cannot be inferred from one sample or from the relation label. If human/nation attacks are meant to be reserved for credible whole conquest, that is a strategic judgment for Moritz and a later evaluated model policy—not a hidden deterministic attack override.
